// 02 — HACKATHON CHALLENGE DOMAINS & PROBLEM TOPICS

FOUR OPERATIONAL
CHALLENGE TRACKS.

Select one specialized operational vector for your Round 1 Proposal Dossier. Each track offers concrete problem prompts spanning quantum simulation, post-quantum security, fault-tolerant error suppression, and open mathematical modeling.

TRACK 01 // QUANTUM GATES ⚛

Quantum Superposition & Circuit Modeling

Investigate state preparation, unitary operators, and quantum simulation of dynamical systems. Teams design efficient circuit topologies, minimize gate depth, and harness variational quantum algorithms.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Hamiltonian State Evolution: Synthesize quantum circuits simulating time-dependent Schrödinger evolution under non-unitary environmental perturbations.
  • ▶ Variational Circuit Optimization: Construct shallow-depth ansatz circuits for VQE molecular energy estimation with minimal CNOT gate overhead.
  • ▶ Multi-Qubit Entanglement Distillation: Formulate state tomography and purification protocols for GHZ / Bell states across noisy quantum channels.
TOOLKITS: Qiskit PennyLane Google Cirq QuTiP NumPy / SciPy
TRACK 02 // CIPHERS & DEFENSE ⛨

Quantum Algorithms, Cryptography & Security

Construct cryptographic shields against quantum cryptanalysis and simulate next-generation quantum communication networks, key distribution, and quantum zero-knowledge proofs.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Post-Quantum Lattice Cryptography: Implement and benchmark ML-KEM (Kyber) and ML-DSA (Dilithium) against quantum Shor's and Grover's search algorithms.
  • ▶ QKD Protocols Under Dephasing Attacks: Simulate BB84 / E91 key exchange across fiber-optic repeaters with real-time intercept-resend attack detection.
  • ▶ Quantum Zero-Knowledge Arguments: Design non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs (ZK-SNARKs / STARKs) resilient to quantum polynomial-time adversaries.
TOOLKITS: liboqs OpenSSL 3.0 PQC SymPy SageMath Python Cryptography
TRACK 03 // ERROR CODES & OPT ⨁

Quantum Error Correction & Optimization

Tackle decoherence and noise in NISQ devices. Develop topological surface codes, syndrome measurement decoders, and combinatorial optimization algorithms like QAOA.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Surface Code Syndrome Decoding: Implement Minimum Weight Perfect Matching (MWPM) or Union-Find decoders on rotated surface codes under high measurement error.
  • ▶ QAOA for Critical Resource Grids: Formulate QUBO / Ising models to solve NP-hard routing, emergency microgrid balancing, and supply-chain logistics.
  • ▶ Dynamical Decoupling & ZNE: Construct Zero-Noise Extrapolation and pulse sequences to mitigate thermal relaxation and dephasing on noisy backend emulators.
TOOLKITS: Stim PyMatching Qiskit Aer Mitiq D-Wave Ocean SDK
TRACK 04 // COMPLEX SYSTEMS ∑

Open Mathematical & Post-Apocalyptic Computing

Harness pure and applied mathematics to solve systemic post-apocalyptic crises. Explore topological data analysis, spectral graph theory, and nonlinear differential modeling.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Topological Data Analysis (TDA): Apply persistent homology, Betti curves, and simplicial complexes to detect structural anomalies in high-dimensional datasets.
  • ▶ Spectral Graph Resilience: Model algebraic connectivity and Fiedler vectors to design self-healing communication topologies resilient to targeted node decimation.
  • ▶ Nonlinear Reaction-Diffusion Dynamics: Simulate atmospheric contaminant dispersal and epidemiological wave fronts using finite difference numerical solvers.
TOOLKITS: GUDHI / Ripser NetworkX SciPy / NumPy Julia DifferentialEquations SymPy
// 03 — ACADEMIC STANDARDS · RULES OF ENGAGEMENT

BUILD WITH
RIGOR & INTEGRITY.

Participation regulations, permitted computational tools, and the five-pillar evaluation rubric. The Department of Mathematics demands mathematical authenticity, scientific rigor, and academic integrity.

⚑ ORGANIZING COMMITTEE DIRECTIVE Official regulations approved and ratified by the Department of Mathematics Organizing Committee.
A / ELIGIBILITY

WHO CAN ENTER

  • Undergraduate and postgraduate students currently enrolled at any recognized college or university.
  • Inter-college participation is strictly NOT allowed. All 4 to 6 members must belong to the exact same institution.
  • Squads must register 4–6 members together at the time of entry, led by one primary contact.
  • Each student may appear on one team roster only. Duplicate registrations are purged automatically.
B / TECHNICAL SCOPE

WHAT TO BUILD & TOOLS

  • Round 1: Formulate a theoretical & algorithmic proposal in one of the 4 designated tracks.
  • Round 2: Shortlisted squads build and simulate working circuits / models in a 24-hr onsite sprint.
  • Permitted Simulators: Qiskit Aer, Google Cirq, PennyLane, Stim, PyMatching, QuTiP, Julia Yao.
  • Show where mathematical formulation directly governs your circuit depth, error mitigation, or optimization.
  • All open-source libraries, pre-trained weights, and mathematical datasets must be strictly cited.
C / INTEGRITY

PLAY FAIR & DEFEND

  • All code, proofs, and circuit architectures must be originally developed by the registered squad.
  • AI scaffolding & coding assistants are permitted for syntax assistance, but mathematical logic must be defended live during jury viva.
  • Plagiarism, fabricated benchmark data, or copied repositories result in immediate disqualification.
  • Maintain collegiate respect toward peer innovators, student volunteers, and academic evaluators.
D / EVALUATION RUBRIC · 5 PILLARS

FIVE LENSES.
