// 01 — ABOUT THE EVENT

ADVANCED MATHEMATICS
MEETS QUANTUM COMPUTING.

Organized by the Department of Mathematics at Patrician College of Arts and Science, QYRON’27 is a national-level hackathon where college students apply mathematical principles and quantum computing to build innovative solutions. Teams work on algorithmic challenges, quantum circuit design, and practical mathematical problem-solving.

// 02 — CHALLENGE TRACKS

FOUR HACKATHON
CHALLENGE TRACKS.

Select one track for your Round 1 proposal presentation. Each track offers problem statements covering quantum simulation, cryptographic security, error correction, and mathematical modeling.

TRACK 01 // QUANTUM GATES ⚛

Quantum Superposition & Circuit Modeling

Investigate state preparation, unitary operators, and quantum simulation of dynamical systems. Teams design efficient circuit topologies, minimize gate depth, and harness variational quantum algorithms.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Hamiltonian State Evolution: Synthesize quantum circuits simulating time-dependent Schrödinger evolution under non-unitary environmental perturbations.
  • ▶ Variational Circuit Optimization: Construct shallow-depth ansatz circuits for VQE molecular energy estimation with minimal CNOT gate overhead.
  • ▶ Multi-Qubit Entanglement Distillation: Formulate state tomography and purification protocols for GHZ / Bell states across noisy quantum channels.
TOOLKITS: Qiskit PennyLane Google Cirq QuTiP NumPy / SciPy
TRACK 02 // CIPHERS & DEFENSE ⛨

Quantum Algorithms, Cryptography & Security

Construct cryptographic shields against quantum cryptanalysis and simulate next-generation quantum communication networks, key distribution, and quantum zero-knowledge proofs.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Post-Quantum Lattice Cryptography: Implement and benchmark ML-KEM (Kyber) and ML-DSA (Dilithium) against quantum Shor's and Grover's search algorithms.
  • ▶ QKD Protocols Under Dephasing Attacks: Simulate BB84 / E91 key exchange across fiber-optic repeaters with real-time intercept-resend attack detection.
  • ▶ Quantum Zero-Knowledge Arguments: Design non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs (ZK-SNARKs / STARKs) resilient to quantum polynomial-time adversaries.
TOOLKITS: liboqs OpenSSL 3.0 PQC SymPy SageMath Python Cryptography
TRACK 03 // ERROR CODES & OPT ⨁

Quantum Error Correction & Optimization

Tackle decoherence and noise in NISQ devices. Develop topological surface codes, syndrome measurement decoders, and combinatorial optimization algorithms like QAOA.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Surface Code Syndrome Decoding: Implement Minimum Weight Perfect Matching (MWPM) or Union-Find decoders on rotated surface codes under high measurement error.
  • ▶ QAOA for Critical Resource Grids: Formulate QUBO / Ising models to solve NP-hard routing, emergency microgrid balancing, and supply-chain logistics.
  • ▶ Dynamical Decoupling & ZNE: Construct Zero-Noise Extrapolation and pulse sequences to mitigate thermal relaxation and dephasing on noisy backend emulators.
TOOLKITS: Stim PyMatching Qiskit Aer Mitiq D-Wave Ocean SDK
TRACK 04 // COMPLEX SYSTEMS ∑

Open Mathematical Modeling & Computing

Apply pure and applied mathematics to solve complex real-world challenges. Explore topological data analysis, spectral graph theory, and nonlinear differential modeling.

CORE PROBLEM TOPICS & CHALLENGES
  • ▶ Topological Data Analysis (TDA): Apply persistent homology, Betti curves, and simplicial complexes to detect structural patterns in high-dimensional datasets.
  • ▶ Spectral Graph Resilience: Model algebraic connectivity and graph theory to design robust communication networks resilient to node failures.
  • ▶ Nonlinear Reaction-Diffusion Dynamics: Simulate complex physical and biological wave fronts using finite difference numerical solvers.
TOOLKITS: GUDHI / Ripser NetworkX SciPy / NumPy Julia DifferentialEquations SymPy
// 03 — RULES & GUIDELINES

BUILD WITH
RIGOR & INTEGRITY.

Participation rules, permitted tools, and the evaluation rubric. The Department of Mathematics emphasizes scientific rigor and academic integrity.

⚑ ORGANIZING COMMITTEE GUIDELINES Official regulations approved by the Department of Mathematics Organizing Committee.
A / ELIGIBILITY

WHO CAN ENTER

  • Undergraduate and postgraduate students currently enrolled at any recognized college or university.
  • Inter-college participation is strictly NOT allowed. All 2 to 4 members must belong to the exact same institution.
  • Teams must register 2–4 members together at the time of entry, led by one team leader.
  • Each student may appear on one team only. Duplicate registrations will be removed.
B / TECHNICAL SCOPE

WHAT TO BUILD & TOOLS

  • Round 1: Submit a theoretical and algorithmic proposal presentation in one of the 4 tracks.
  • Round 2: Shortlisted teams will build and simulate their projects during a 24-hour on-campus hackathon.
  • Permitted Simulators: Qiskit Aer, Google Cirq, PennyLane, Stim, PyMatching, QuTiP, Julia Yao.
  • Highlight how mathematical formulation directly supports your solution design and performance.
  • All open-source libraries, pre-trained models, and external datasets must be properly cited.
C / INTEGRITY

FAIR PLAY & INTEGRITY

  • All code, proofs, and solutions must be original work created by the registered team.
  • AI tools and coding assistants may be used for syntax support, but all mathematical logic must be clearly explained during the presentation.
  • Plagiarism, fabricated benchmark data, or copied projects will result in immediate disqualification.
  • Maintain collegiate respect toward fellow participants, student volunteers, and judges.
D / EVALUATION RUBRIC · 6 CRITERIA
